## 7. Stress narrative — the worst drawdown (`ranked_L3`, 2024-09-27 → 2025-02-28, −22.25%)

Figures are from §5 above and `stress_*.csv`. The episode is the FPI-outflow correction, not March 2020, and it
had **not recovered** by the end of the window. NIFTY 50 TRI fell 15.15% over the same dates, so at 3× the book
lost about 1.5× the index. NAV went ₹6.09 cr → ₹4.74 cr, a ₹1.36 cr loss over five expiries.

**Positions going in.** At the September 2024 expiry close the book was nearly all cash: ₹4.46 cr of ₹6.09 cr
NAV, 8.9% margin use, two stock holdings (TATAMOTORS ₹81 L and RELIANCE ₹82 L, both with covered calls) and
**zero** open short puts. NIFTY had made an all-time high on 2024-09-26. The next day the rules filled a fresh
full book of 4%-OTM cash-secured puts at 3× notional across the top-ranked names, all sharing the October expiry.

**Month-end NAV (₹ cr).** 6.06 (Sep) → 5.22 (Oct, −14% in one expiry) → 5.24 → 5.13 → 5.06 → 4.74 (Feb).
Margin utilisation ran from 8.9% at the peak to a 78.7% high inside the episode and 32.5% at the trough.

**Decisions the rules forced.**
1. **The October 2024 expiry (2024-10-31)** — the single event that made the drawdown. Many puts finished ITM
   together.
   - Six were **delivered**: M&M, SHRIRAMFIN, POWERGRID, BAJFINANCE, AXISBANK and NTPC, at gaps of 1.7–10.5%
     below the strike. Paying the strikes cost ₹5.00 cr of cash, which is what took utilisation to 79%.
   - Six more had no cash behind them under Step 9a and were **closed at intrinsic** (`put_forced_realisation`)
     for ₹21.3 L: BAJAJFINSV ₹7.6 L, TATASTEEL ₹4.9 L, HINDALCO ₹3.0 L, ULTRACEMCO ₹2.7 L, BHARTIARTL ₹2.5 L,
     SUNPHARMA ₹0.5 L.
   - Three puts never reached expiry: the **15% stop** bought back BAJAJ-AUTO (17 Oct), NESTLEIND (24 Oct) and
     MARUTI (29 Oct) after the underlying closed 15% below its level on the day the put was sold. Those three
     are the bulk of the ₹33.3 L of buy-backs in the episode.
2. **Stock-heavy months, few new puts.** With delivered shares waiting for covered calls, six `SKIP_NEW_PUT`
   decisions (Oct, Nov, Dec, Jan, Feb ×2) held back new premium — each with 1–7 uncovered names pending. Premium
   sold over the whole five months was only ₹33.9 L, against ₹5.00 cr paid out on strikes.
3. **The call floor left the delivered shares naked.** The economic-basis floor (no call below the delivered
   basis) produced 147 `covered_call_not_available` days, concentrated in NTPC (46), TATAMOTORS (46),
   SHRIRAMFIN (25) and AXISBANK (21). Those names fell while nothing was written against them.
4. **Later expiries.** January 2025 delivered DRREDDY and ADANIPORTS; February delivered BEL. Call-aways
   returned ₹83.3 L of cash (BAJFINANCE in January, ADANIPORTS and BEL in March, just after the trough).

**Where the ₹1.36 cr went.**
- **Delivery-leg price P&L is nearly all of it.** The book went from 2 stock names to 10 (₹4.62 cr of stock at
  the trough) by paying strikes 2–10% above where the shares then traded, and several kept falling: M&M's low
  was ₹2,491 against a ₹3,050 strike, SHRIRAMFIN ₹512 against ₹690, POWERGRID ₹251 against ₹340.
- **Worst names, peak → trough:** TATAMOTORS −₹29.4 L (held right through), POWERGRID −₹17.8 L,
  RELIANCE −₹16.2 L, BAJAJ-AUTO −₹13.5 L (mostly its stop-loss buy-back), MARUTI −₹12.6 L, AXISBANK −₹11.5 L,
  DRREDDY −₹11.4 L. Only five names were positive, none above ₹1.8 L.
- **Realised option losses:** ₹21.3 L of intrinsic on the six unfunded puts, plus ₹33.3 L of buy-backs.
- **What came in:** premium ₹33.9 L, T-bill interest ₹3.3 L, dividends ₹2.5 L — together about 29% of the loss.
- **Costs:** ₹2.5 L, of which ₹2.05 L is delivery and call-away charges.

**What the episode says.** The damage is a full book of same-expiry short puts opened the day after an all-time
high, and then a stock book the rules would not write calls against. The 15% stop cut three of them early but
cost more than it saved inside this window; the cash-only rule (Step 9a) is what turned six more into realised
losses rather than deliveries. Moving the put further OTM is the one lever in §6 that consistently shrinks this
episode: at put 6% / call 5% the L3 drawdown is −17.1% instead of −22.3%.

## 8. Sensitivity — fragility versus robustness

Tables are in §6. Two alternative strike rules (put 2% / call 2% and put 6% / call 5%, around the
put 4% / call 3% base) and four alternative slippage levels around `base`.

**Robust:**
- **L1 and L2 barely move.** Across both strike alternatives and the zero-to-high slippage levels, L1's CAGR
  stays in 9.4–11.1% and L2's in 13.1–17.3%. Neither worst drawdown exceeds 20.0% in any variant.
- **The strike offset behaves monotonically at L1–L3.** Tighter strikes buy return with drawdown and looser
  strikes sell it back, in order, at every one of the three leverages that matter:

  | offsets | L3 CAGR | L3 MDD | L3 Sharpe |
  |:--|--:|--:|--:|
  | put 2% / call 2% | 19.23% | −30.09% | 0.82 |
  | **put 4% / call 3% (base)** | **18.05%** | **−22.25%** | **0.91** |
  | put 6% / call 5% | 16.94% | −17.08% | 0.97 |

  The base is the middle of a smooth trade-off, not a peak found by search. Risk-adjusted, the looser rule is
  actually the better one (Sharpe 0.97, Calmar higher still); the base is chosen for return per unit of the
  drawdown the mandate tolerates, and nothing here depends on the exact 4/3 split — ±1 point moves CAGR by
  roughly 1 point in the expected direction.
- **Slippage direction is right at L2–L3.** Every step from `zero` to `stress` lowers CAGR at L3
  (17.93 → 15.44%), and `stress` (8 ticks / 10% of premium per leg) costs 2.5 points there against 1.3 at L1.
  Drawdowns widen monotonically at L1–L4. The strategy survives a punitive fill assumption: L3 still returns
  15.4% against the index's 12.2%.

**Fragile:**
- **L4 and L5 do not respond smoothly.**
  - At L5, CAGR goes 21.49% / 15.86% / 17.14% for the tight / base / loose strike rules. The base sits in a hole
    between both neighbours — that is not a function, it is path noise.
  - On slippage, L4 earns *more* at `zero`→`low`→`base` non-monotonically (19.65 / 19.15 / 17.60%), and L5 earns
    more at `base` (15.86%) than at `low` (14.31%). A parameter that makes fills worse should never raise return.
  - The mechanism is Step 9a: at high leverage a small parameter change decides whether a name had cash behind it
    at the October 2024 expiry, and therefore whether it was realised at intrinsic or delivered and later
    recovered. One expiry dominates the whole path.
- **Read L4–L5 as one draw from a wide distribution**, not point estimates. The headline is L3
  (params.yaml `leverage: 3.0`). At L3 both parameters move CAGR the expected way.
  `final_results/README.md` §4 (the pre-stop version is in `archive/superseded_docs/`) has the full put × call grid. The rank threshold is the more sensitive knob
  (`docs/STOCK_SELECTION.md`: 1.5 → 1.0 costs 6.8 CAGR points at L5).
- **Conclusion:** the wheel's edge at 1–3× is stable across every parameter tested here — the ordering of
  CAGR, drawdown and Sharpe against both the strike offset and slippage is the one theory predicts. Above 3×
  the results are driven by the path, and the parameter grid cannot tell skill from luck.

## 9. Remaining biases and known imperfections

**Look-ahead**
- *Trades:* none found. Every decision uses data up to the close of day t and fills at t+1's close. The rulebook's
  look-ahead checks (decisions unchanged when data after t is deleted) are in the test suite (`tests/test_stage2_engine.py`, `test_stage3_real.py`). The FSP is the CM
  close on expiry day, known only after the fact, and it is used only to settle.
- *Parameters:* this is the real look-ahead.
  - `rank_threshold` = 1.5 was chosen from the exercise-rate curve over the whole 2019–2026 window, and the
    backtest then runs on that same window (`docs/STOCK_SELECTION.md` §3).
  - `n_positions` = 20 was not chosen from results, but it was never tested out of sample.
  - The strike offsets (put 4% / call 3%) **were** set after the put × call grid in `final_results/README.md` §4 (the pre-stop version is in `archive/superseded_docs/`)
    had been run over the whole window. §8 shows the L3 trade-off is monotonic, so this is a choice of a point on
    a known curve rather than a peak fitted to noise — but it is in-sample either way.
  - `put_stop_loss_pct` = 0.15 and `leverage` = 3.0 were likewise fixed on 2026-09-18, after the L1–L5 grid and
    `stop_loss_probe.py` had been seen. Both are disclosed in params.yaml.
  - The L4 run and the `zero` and `stress` slippage levels were added after the first results were seen. They are
    reported as sensitivity only and did not change any headline parameter.

**Settlement-price realism**
- *FSP:* taken as NSE's rule, the CM close on expiry day. Checked against the expiring future's settle on 4,625
  (expiry, name) pairs: median difference 0.23 bps, max 20.9 bps, **zero** assignments or call-aways change
  (`FSP_CHECK.md`).
- *Delivery costs:* charged on max(K, FSP) × qty, which is conservative.
- *The weakest assumption is how an unfunded ITM put is closed.*
  - The engine closes it at intrinsic value at the FSP.
  - In practice stock options must be physically settled. Brokers raise delivery margin through expiry week and
    square off unfunded ITM positions **before** expiry, at a price that still has time value and spread.
  - So the 41 cash-settled puts at L5 (32 at L4, 27 at L3, 9 at L2, none at L1) are probably **flattered**.
- *Expiry-week delivery margin (physical-settlement ramp):* not modelled either. That understates the capital
  locked up in the last week before expiry.
- *Fills:* use the EOD close of strikes that traded at least 10 contracts, capped at 10% of the day's volume, with
  explicit slippage. Marks on untraded strikes use NSE's settle price, which is theoretical and at 3.9% of
  adjacent-strike pairs not even arbitrage-free.

**Survivorship**
- *Universe:* point-in-time NIFTY 50 membership rebuilt from NSE Indices releases. Delisted and demoted names
  (YESBANK, ZEEL, VEDL, IBULHSGFIN, HDFC, INFRATEL) stay selectable while they were members (`docs/DATA.md` §4.5).
- *Residual (U-11):* HDFC, INFRATEL, LTIM and ZEEL have no 15-minute bars for 84 member-expiries, so they could not
  be selected then. A proxy re-run found the bias small and slightly flattering, about 0.1–1 CAGR point, mainly
  because ZEEL is excluded just before its collapse.
  - Those U-11 figures were measured before the Step 9a cash-only rule. They should be re-run against the current
    engine before being quoted.
- *Buy-and-hold benchmark:* only **5** names cleared the rank threshold on 2019-12-31, a metals-and-energy basket.
  It is a fair "names the rule picked" benchmark but a narrow one. The rebalanced top-20 series is the broader
  comparison.

**Other known imperfections**
- **Cash interest is a large share of low-leverage returns.**
  - Free cash earns the 3-month T-bill: ₹70.2 L of L1's ₹1.54 cr gain, and ₹49 L at L5.
  - A broker pays nothing on idle margin. Earning this needs liquid funds or T-bills pledged as collateral, with
    haircuts and a 50% cash-collateral rule.
  - Without it, L1's CAGR falls by roughly 3.5 points, to about 5.5%.
- **The margin model is simplified.** Margin = strike × qty / L, not SPAN + exposure. There are no exchange margin
  hikes in stress (NSE raised them in March 2020) and no freeze-quantity order splitting.
- **Costs are backdated.** Broker charges are Zerodha's 2026 schedule applied from 2019. Before July 2020, state
  stamp duties are replaced by the uniform post-reform rate. All results are pre-tax: no STCG/LTCG, and no tax on
  dividends or interest.
- **Corporate actions.** The JIOFIN, ITC Hotels and Kwality Wall's demerger values are assumed to be realised at
  discovery or reference prices. The BRITANNIA 2021 bonus debenture is not modelled (`docs/CORPORATE_ACTIONS.md` §7–8).
- **One path, one window.** 6.5 years, with the March 2020 crash in the first quarter while the book was still
  small. The worst episode (§7) is a single expiry. With 78 monthly expiries, the tails, and so the leverage
  choice, rest on very few independent events.
